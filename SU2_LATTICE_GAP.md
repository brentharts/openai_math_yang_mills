# SU(2) lattice gauge theory on small systems: a computed gap

A fork-level note, not part of the OpenAI release. It records the lattice-side statements for SU(2) that the fork computes to full precision, in three systems: a two-dimensional theory, a single plaquette and two plaquettes sharing a link. It says what each does and does not give.

**Nothing on this page proves, supports or bears on Yang-Mills existence or a mass gap in the continuum.** The two-dimensional theory is exactly solvable and has no mass gap in the infinite-volume sense. The one-plaquette system has a single degree of freedom, so a positive gap there is automatic, and the two-plaquette ladder has six. See [YANGMILLS.md](YANGMILLS.md) for the status of the reported claim.

## Why this page exists

[YM_CONNECTIONS.md](YM_CONNECTIONS.md) listed, as item 3 of "What would be progress", a lattice-side exact statement for SU(2) "in the form of a finite-volume transfer-matrix gap that can be computed exactly for a small lattice and compared with the continuum scaling. This is a numerical bridge, not a proof." This page treats the cases where the answer is available in closed form or to machine precision, with every number rederived by two models: [`su2_lattice_gap.py`](sympy_models/models/su2_lattice_gap.py) for Parts A and B (34 checks) and [`su2_lattice_ladder.py`](sympy_models/models/su2_lattice_ladder.py) for Part C (17 checks), both in entry `su2-lattice-gap`.

The comparison with the continuum scaling is **not** achieved. None of the three systems has a scaling window. The last sections say why and what would.

## Related work

The fork's strong-coupling lattice results are Tristan Romanov's: [yang-mills-3d-forest-gauge](https://github.com/romanov360/yang-mills-3d-forest-gauge) (a mass bound and a spectral-gap range for SU(2) on `Z³` in a period-3 forest gauge) and [yang-mills-4d-forest-certificate](https://github.com/romanov360/yang-mills-4d-forest-certificate) (its four-dimensional sequel, after Bhattacharjee, Bhattacharya and Lockwood), checked in [`forest_gauge.py`](sympy_models/models/forest_gauge.py) and [`forest_gauge_4d.py`](sympy_models/models/forest_gauge_4d.py) and described in [YM_CONNECTIONS.md](YM_CONNECTIONS.md) (row 1). **This page does not use his theorems, certificates or code.** The one idea it shares with his work is gauge fixing along a spanning tree so that only a few loop variables remain: that is how Part B reduces four links to one holonomy and Part C reduces seven links to two. His forest gauge does this for an infinite periodic Euclidean lattice, to prove uniqueness and decay at strong coupling; here it is a finite Hamiltonian system, to compute a spectrum. The two are related in method only, and nothing in Parts A to C should be read as an application of his results.

## Conventions

- Euclidean: Wilson weight `exp((β/2) Tr U)` per plaquette, `β = 4/g²`.
- Hamiltonian (Kogut–Susskind 1975): `H = (g²/2) Σ_links E² + (1/g²) Σ_plaquettes (2 − Tr U_p)`, with `E² = j(j+1)` on a link of spin `j`, `Tr` in the fundamental, lattice units.
- Other texts differ by factors of 2 in `g²`. The strong-coupling gap `3g²/2` below fixes the convention.

## Part A. Two dimensions: exact string tension

The character coefficients of the plaquette weight are `(1/d_j) ∫ dU w χ_j = (2/β) I_{2j+1}(β)` with `d_j = 2j+1`. A ring of `L` plaquettes then has transfer-matrix eigenvalues `(I_{2j+1}/I_1)^L`, so

- the string tension is `σ(β) = −ln(I₂(β)/I₁(β)) = 3/(2β) + 3/(4β²) + 3/(16β³) − 9/(16β⁴) + O(β⁻⁵)`,
- the ring levels are `E_j(L) = −L ln(I_{2j+1}/I₁)`, linear in `L`,
- the torus partition function is `Z = Σ_j (2I_{2j+1}(β)/β)^A` for `A` plaquettes.

| β | g² = 4/β | σ(β) | 3/(2β) | four-term series | ln(I₃/I₁) / ln(I₂/I₁) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 4 | 1.4263 | 1.5000 | 1.875 | 2.2705 |
| 2 | 2 | 0.8367 | 0.7500 | 0.9258 | 2.4044 |
| 4 | 1 | 0.4185 | 0.3750 | 0.4226 | 2.5642 |
| 8 | 0.5 | 0.19937 | 0.18750 | 0.19945 | 2.6434 |
| 16 | 0.25 | 0.09672 | 0.09375 | 0.09672 | 2.6616 |

The last column tends to `8/3 = C₂(1)/C₂(½)`: Casimir scaling, exact in two dimensions. The large-β series is asymptotic and poor at small β, as the first rows show.

What this says: the theory is confining at every coupling, with an area law, and the gap on a ring grows as `Lσ`. **It has no finite infinite-volume gap and no dimensional transmutation.** The continuum limit exists trivially (the theory is super-renormalizable) and is linear confinement with `σ = 3g²/8` in lattice units. This is the opposite of the two-dimensional O(3) model, whose gap is finite and generated by asymptotic freedom, and the contrast is worth keeping in view: four-dimensional Yang-Mills is expected, though not proved, to combine both features, the confinement of the first and the dynamically generated scale of the second.

## Part B. One plaquette: a Mathieu spectrum

One plaquette with open boundary has four links and four vertices. Gauge invariance leaves class functions of the plaquette holonomy, spanned by the characters `χ_j`, with every link in the same spin `j`. With `k = 2j+1 = 1, 2, …`,

```
H_kk = (g²/2)(k² − 1) + 2/g²,        H_{k,k±1} = −1/g²
```

Writing the states as `u(θ) = sin θ · χ(θ)` turns this into `H u = −(g²/2)(u'' + u) + (2/g²)(1 − cos θ) u` on `[0, π]` with `u(0) = u(π) = 0`, which is Mathieu's equation:

```
E_k = 2/g² − g²/2 + (g²/8) b_{2k}(8/g⁴),        Δ(g) = E_{j=1/2} − E_{j=0} = (g²/8) [b₄(q) − b₂(q)],   q = 8/g⁴
```

The character matrix and the Mathieu matrix are the same matrix (diagonal `4m²`, off-diagonal `q`) after a shift and a scale; the model checks this symbolically.

### Three independent computations of the gap

| Method | Agreement with the character matrix |
|---|---|
| tridiagonal character matrix, truncated at `K = 40, 120, 400` characters | the reference; the gap changes by at most `1.2e-11` between `K = 160` and `K = 320` |
| finite differences in `θ`, Richardson-extrapolated | `2.8e-9` over `g² ∈ [0.05, 8]`, four lowest levels |
| scipy's `mathieu_b(2k, 8/g⁴)` | `4.3e-13` over `g² ∈ [0.1, 8]`, four lowest levels |

scipy's Mathieu routine is not reliable at `q ≈ 3000` (`g² = 0.05`), so that comparison starts at `g² = 0.1`, `q = 800`. The other two agree down to `g² = 0.05`.

Blank cells are where the expansion does not converge or is not useful. The bold row is the minimum, where neither expansion is any good.

- **Strong coupling.** `Δ = 2g²[3/4 + (28/15)ε² − (10072/3375)ε⁴ + (40810352/5315625)ε⁶ − …]` with `ε = 1/(2g⁴)`. The coefficients are exact rationals from a continued-fraction recursion; the second-order value `28/15` was also found by hand (`4/3` for the ground state through `j = ½`, plus `4/3 − 4/5` for the excited state through `j = 0` and `j = 1`). Odd orders vanish. The ratios of successive even coefficients rise from `1.6` to `4.2` over the 14 orders, which suggests a finite radius of convergence near `g² ≈ 1`. That is an estimate from seven coefficients, not a proof.
- **Weak coupling.** Near `U = 1` the class angle is an oscillator of mass `1/g²` and frequency `√2` that does not depend on `g`, restricted to odd states, so `Δ → 2·√2 = 2.828`. The quartic term of the cosine gives `−5g²/8` at first order (exact Hermite integrals: `−5g²/32` and `−25g²/32` for the two states). The next term, `−41√2 g⁴/512`, comes from the large-`q` expansion of Mathieu's equation (NIST DLMF §28.8) and is checked numerically to within 2% at `g² = 0.01` and `0.02`; it is not rederived by perturbation theory.
- **The whole curve.** `Δ` falls from `2√2` at weak coupling to a minimum of `2.1962` at `g² ≈ 0.9875`, then rises as `3g²/2`. It is positive on 400 values of `g²` from 0.01 to 1000, which Sturm–Liouville theory guarantees (simple eigenvalues of a regular problem on a bounded interval; quoted, not reproved).

## Part C. Two plaquettes: the first volume dependence

Two plaquettes sharing one link, open boundary: seven links, six vertices. Gauge fixing along a spanning tree leaves two plaquette holonomies `U_a`, `U_b`. The three outer links of each plaquette carry one spin, `j_a` and `j_b`, and the shared link carries `J`, with the triangle rule and `j_a + j_b + J` an integer. These are the spin networks of the theta graph, `Ψ = Tr[P_J (D^{j_a}(U_a) ⊗ D^{j_b}(U_b))]` with norm² `d_J/(d_a d_b)`, and

```
H = (g²/2)[3 j_a(j_a+1) + 3 j_b(j_b+1) + J(J+1)] + (1/g²)(4 − W_a − W_b)
⟨j_a±½, j_b, J±½ | W_a | j_a, j_b, J⟩ = √(d_{j_a} d_{j_a'} d_J d_{J'}) · {j_a ½ j_a'; J' j_b J}²
```

and `W_b` the same with `a` and `b` exchanged. The 6j symbol enters **squared**: the left and right index sets of the loop wavefunction carry the same recoupling. (A first guess with the symbol unsquared failed the check against explicit Clebsch–Gordan contractions, which is what pointed to the square.) For one entry equal to `½`, the squares have the closed forms of Edmonds' Table 5, so the matrix is built for tens of thousands of states in seconds. When `j_b = 0` the matrix element is 1 and the one-plaquette chain is recovered.

The matrix elements are tested three ways: against sympy's exact 6j symbols (969 transitions, exact equality); against an independent construction from explicit Clebsch–Gordan contractions (500 transitions to `3·10⁻¹⁶`); and by the fact that `W_a` and `W_b`, which are multiplication operators and must commute, commute exactly on the whole truncated space, with the reflection `a ↔ b` mapping one to the other.

### Weak coupling

Near `U = 1` each plaquette angle is a three-dimensional oscillator. The electric energy is `(g²/2)[3E_a² + 3E_b² + (E_a − E_b)²]`, so the kinetic matrix is `[[4, −1], [−1, 4]]` with eigenvalues 3 (symmetric mode) and 5. The frequencies are `ω = √(eigenvalue/2)`: `ω_s = √(3/2)`, `ω_d = √(5/2)`. Gauge invariance keeps total angular momentum 0, so the lowest excitation is the radial one in the soft mode, `2ω_s = √6`, then `ω_s + ω_d` (odd under `a ↔ b`) and `2ω_d = √10`.

| | predicted | numerical, extrapolated from `g² = 0.05, 0.1` to 0 | reflection parity |
|---|---|---|---|
| `2ω_s` | `√6 = 2.4495` | 2.4499 | even |
| `ω_s + ω_d` | `√(3/2) + √(5/2) = 2.8059` | 2.8065 | odd |
| `2ω_d` | `√10 = 3.1623` | 3.1619 | even |

The prediction was made before the ladder was solved and agrees to `6·10⁻⁴`, including the parities. Only the limit is checked; the first correction in `g²` is not derived. The ratio to the one-plaquette limit is `√6 / (2√2) = √3/2`.

**Prediction for a strip of `N` plaquettes (not verified for `N ≥ 3`).** The same argument gives a kinetic matrix `4 − (adjacency of the path)`, lowest eigenvalue `4 − 2cos(π/(N+1))`, and weak-coupling gap `2√(2 − cos(π/(N+1)))`: `2√2` for `N = 1`, `√6` for `N = 2` (both verified here), tending to 2 as `N → ∞`. This is the harmonic approximation of a strip of width one, not a continuum statement.

### Strong coupling

The first excited level is a string around plaquette `a` or around plaquette `b`, degenerate at `3g²/2` and coupled only at second order. Exact degenerate perturbation theory (rational arithmetic) splits it into an even and an odd combination under `a ↔ b`:

```
Δ_+ = 3g²/2 + (26/35)/g⁶ + O(g⁻¹⁴)     (even)
Δ_− = 3g²/2 + (14/15)/g⁶ + O(g⁻¹⁴)     (odd)
```

The odd coefficient `14/15` is exactly the one-plaquette value. The even combination differs because it lies in the same sector as the vacuum, which pushes it up through the direct coupling `W_a + W_b`; the odd one cannot mix with it. The vacuum itself moves down by `4/(3g⁶)`. At `g² = 8` the numerical odd gap is `12.001823`, equal to the one-plaquette gap to six digits, and the even gap is `12.001451`, matching the formula to the digits shown. (An earlier version of the derivation left the vacuum out of the intermediate states. The numbers, which disagreed with the even state by exactly `4/(3g⁶)` and not with the odd one, are what showed it.)

Like the single plaquette, the ladder gap has a minimum near `g² ≈ 1` (about 2.10, from a coarse scan between 0.5 and 1.6). This is a volume dependence for two volumes, which is a start and not a trend.

## What this does not say

- It makes no statement about existence or a mass gap of Yang-Mills on `ℝ⁴`. The systems are a two-dimensional gauge theory and two small quantum-mechanical systems, with one and two class-function variables (one plaquette holonomy, two plaquette holonomies).
- Part A has no mass gap in the infinite-volume sense and no running coupling. Part B's positive gap holds at every coupling for a trivial reason.
- Part B is a single plaquette in lattice units. It has no volume dependence and no continuum limit. At weak coupling the gap tends to the constant `2√2`, not to zero, so there is no region where it follows the exponential law `m ~ e^{−1/(2b₀g²)}` of [YANGMILLS.md](YANGMILLS.md). The O(N) comparison in [`explore/ym_mass_gap.py`](sympy_models/explore/ym_mass_gap.py) cannot be extended to it.
- The `g⁴` coefficient of the weak-coupling expansion rests on a textbook expansion and a numerical check, not on a second derivation.
- Part C has no scaling window either: the ladder gap tends to `√6` at weak coupling, not to zero. Two volumes do not make a trend, and the `N`-plaquette strip formula is a harmonic prediction that has been tested only for `N = 1, 2`.
- The first weak-coupling correction in `g²` for the ladder is not derived, and its minimum (about 2.10 near `g² ≈ 1`) comes from a coarse scan, not a root-find.
- Open boundary conditions throughout (four links for Part B, seven for Part C). A periodic or larger system has a different Hilbert space and different numbers.

## Reading it against the O(N) results

Three comparisons are available and one is not.

1. **Two dimensions against two dimensions.** The O(3) paper builds a theory whose gap is finite in infinite volume and appears through asymptotic freedom. Part A is the gauge-theory counterpart in the same dimension, and it is the opposite: confinement with a gap growing like `L`. Neither says anything about four dimensions, but together they indicate what a four-dimensional theory is expected (not proved) to do: confine like the first and generate a scale like the second.
2. **Strong to weak coupling.** In Part B the strong-coupling expansion and the weak-coupling expansion bracket the exact curve, each failing near the minimum at `g² ≈ 1`. The strong-coupling end is the regime where the Dobrushin and Hessian results quoted in [`forest_gauge.py`](sympy_models/models/forest_gauge.py) apply on `Z³`. Those results are Tristan Romanov's ([yang-mills-3d-forest-gauge](https://github.com/romanov360/yang-mills-3d-forest-gauge), Zenodo [10.5281/zenodo.23783317](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23783317)); they are different statements about a different system (an infinite Euclidean `Z³` lattice, proved) and this page neither uses nor reproduces them, and nothing here connects the two.
3. **Volume dependence, first step.** The ladder shows that the strong-coupling string excitation does not feel the second plaquette at leading order (the odd combination has exactly the one-plaquette `14/15`), and that at weak coupling the gap drops by `√3/2`. This is the same qualitative pattern as a finite-size effect that vanishes at strong coupling and grows at weak coupling, but two volumes cannot support a scaling statement.
4. **Not available: a scaling window.** A comparison with the O(N) local slopes of `ln m` needs a gap in lattice units that tends to zero as the coupling tends to its critical value, which needs a volume large enough to hold a correlation length. One or two plaquettes cannot hold one.

## What would be next

- **Three plaquettes**, to test the strip formula `2√(2 − cos(π/(N+1)))`: at `N = 3` it predicts `2√(2 − 1/√2) ≈ 2.2741`. The vertex recoupling needs more than one 6j per plaquette operator, so this is a real extension of Part C, not a rerun.
- **Larger open or periodic lattices** by the same recursion, with truncation convergence checked as here. The Hilbert space grows quickly, so the practical reach is a handful of plaquettes.
- **A figure** of `Δ(g²)` with the two expansions, in the manner of Figures 5 and 6 of the concise paper (`sympy_models/pictures.py`).
